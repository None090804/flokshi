## The nerds community has a backup plan in case Linus Torvalds gets run over by a bus.

I remember being like 19 something, when I first read about who Linus Torvalds was, (yeah pretty old, not getting any time younger though), in my operating system class. Old, Finnish computer science guy who created Linux. I thought it was cool and connected to the net to find out more about it. A couple years later I still connect to the net to learn about Linux, like everyday. That is how amazing and for me it is just intuitive. Nothing more to say. Back to when I was 19, I learned that the nerds of linux were pretty scared what would happen once Linus was retired or worse, but them being nerds, they described it as: "What would happen to Linux if Linus is to be run over by a bus", yeah. Remember the meme about being in the school campus and potentially getting run over by the bus, yeah the Linux nerds also feared that. 
> I know that this sounds like a stupid fear to be afraid of, but trust me, this is the biggest fear of the nerds community

Linux has been active since 1991. Just a hobby, won't be big and professional like gnu, as Linus explained himself, in the famous email(Figure 1). Yeah 35 years later, and 2026 is the year of Linux in Desktop, since you know.. windows :|.

> MINIX is a Unix-like operating system based on a microkernel architecture, first released in 1987 and written by American-Dutch computer scientist Andrew S. Tanenbaum. It is a predecessor of Linux. The man who created it was Andrew S. Tanenbaum, whose books I strongly suggest you to read. 

Back to the bus story. So yeah the nerds went that far, that to support their hypothesis they even conducted a study. They conducted a lot of studies, but one from the year 2000, was like the best I have seen so far. The following: https://www.crummy.com/writing/segfault.org/Bus.html blog conducted a statistical study on the chances that Linus Torvalds has to survive if he gets hit by a bus. 

*Strongly recommend reading the blog. It is quite nice*

The author concludes the study:

> Our study's bottom line: given standard traffic patterns, Linus Torvalds has an 8.9% (plus or minus 1.4%) chance of surviving and fully recovering from a collision with a bus. Due to Torvalds' development skills and acknowledged importance to the Linux community, we hereby recommend that Linus Torvalds be kept out of the path of oncoming buses. Also, his muffin intake should be monitored.

So yeah. And I think their plan has generally shown results since it has been 25 years later and Linus is still highly active with the kernel. I mean look at his commit history. 

But now, since Linus and the rest of the community are getting **gray** and **old**, a formal plan has been conducted. This morning I came across the following blog: https://www.tomshardware.com/software/linux/linux-kernel-community-draws-up-contingency-plan-to-replace-linus-torvalds-should-the-need-arise-only-34-years-in-the-making, which emphasizes the following 

> Despite expressing some concern about the number of maintainers earlier in the decade, he's not currently concerned about the technical acumen of the talent pool, either, going as far as saying that "it's not instant, but there are new people who come in, and three years later they are a main developer."

> After all, as Torvalds himself remarked in the past, there are not a lot of open-source projects that "have maintainers that have literally been around for over three decades".

The community is old, but strong. 
Another funny thing in the post was that the `bus factor` was still present. A weird obsession does the community have with buses, but hey. As long as there is an open source project, there will also be a community to support it, that is the beauty
